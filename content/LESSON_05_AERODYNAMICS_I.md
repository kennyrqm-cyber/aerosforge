# Lesson 05 — Aerodynamics I

## Editorial position
This is where AEROSFORGE earns credibility. Make the invisible visible: rotor thrust, airflow, induced flow, translational effects, and control inputs. Use conceptual models and original animation rather than copied textbook figures. Never oversimplify a safety-critical aerodynamic phenomenon into a universal rule.

## Master video
**Working title:** Helicopter Aerodynamics Without the Headache

**Hook:** The helicopter is constantly creating and reacting to airflow. If you can visualize what the rotor is doing to the air—and what the air is doing back—you can understand why helicopter performance and handling change.

**Core beats:**
1. Rotor blades act as rotating airfoils and generate aerodynamic force.
2. The direction of the rotor’s aerodynamic force can be changed through the rotor-control system.
3. Induced flow/downwash is part of the rotor’s operating environment.
4. Hovering is aerodynamically demanding because the rotor operates in the air it has accelerated downward and outward.
5. Forward flight changes the rotor’s airflow environment and can improve efficiency through translational effects.
6. Advancing/retreating blade conditions are not identical in forward flight; rotorcraft aerodynamics must account for asymmetric airflow.
7. Density, weight, wind, temperature, altitude, and aircraft configuration can affect performance.
8. AEROSFORGE should teach the “why,” then send learners to the aircraft manual and CFI for aircraft-specific application.

## Short-form hooks
- “The air is moving even when the helicopter looks still.”
- “Why is hovering so weird?”
- “The rotor creates its own airflow.”
- “One rotor disk. Two very different blade environments.”
- “This is why helicopter aerodynamics gets serious fast.”

## Gauntlet seed
Give learners two otherwise similar takeoff scenarios with different environmental conditions. Ask which information must be checked before deciding whether the planned operation remains appropriate. The goal is disciplined performance thinking, not a one-size-fits-all answer.

## Quiz seeds
- What does a rotor blade fundamentally act like aerodynamically?
- Why does hovering create a challenging airflow environment?
- What changes when a helicopter transitions into forward flight?
- Name several factors that can affect helicopter performance.

## Oral-exam seed
“Explain induced flow to a student who has never flown.”

## Rights / safety gate
All diagrams/animations should be original or rights-cleared. Avoid copying FAA textbook illustrations. Use FAA references to fact-check concepts, then build AEROSFORGE’s own explanatory model.
