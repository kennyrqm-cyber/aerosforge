# AEROSFORGE™ Content Package — Lesson 01

## Lesson
Helicopter Fundamentals

## Editorial objective
Give a complete beginner a clear mental model of what makes a helicopter different from a fixed-wing aircraft, without pretending that a short media lesson replaces formal flight instruction.

## Master video concept
**Title:** Helicopters Explained: The 7 Things Every New Pilot Must Understand
**Hook:** A helicopter does not simply “fly like an airplane with a rotor.” Seven systems and aerodynamic ideas determine whether it can hover, climb, cruise, or descend safely.
**Structure:** 1) rotor creates lift, 2) cyclic controls attitude/translation, 3) collective changes rotor thrust, 4) pedals manage yaw/anti-torque, 5) engine/transmission deliver power, 6) helicopter performance changes with conditions, 7) pilot decisions matter as much as aircraft capability.
**CTA:** Start AEROSFORGE Academy Lesson 01, then enter The Gauntlet.

## Short-form hooks
1. “Why can a helicopter hover when an airplane can’t?”
2. “Four controls. One helicopter. Do you know what each actually does?”
3. “The rotor is not just a propeller pointed upward.”
4. “Before you ever fly a helicopter, understand this.”
5. “Pilot question: what changes when you pull collective?”

## Gauntlet seed
**Scenario:** You are a new student preparing for your first helicopter lesson. The aircraft is parked and running. Your instructor asks you to identify the purpose of the cyclic, collective, and pedals before touching a control.
**Decision:** Explain the control effect first; do not manipulate controls without instructor direction.
**Learning objective:** Control-function recognition and disciplined cockpit behavior.

## Quiz seeds
- Which control primarily changes rotor thrust?
- Which control primarily manages helicopter pitch/roll attitude?
- What is the primary purpose of anti-torque pedals?
- Why is aircraft performance context-dependent?

## Oral-exam seed
“Explain the basic functions of cyclic, collective, and anti-torque pedals to a non-pilot.”

## Carousel
Slide 1: Helicopters are controllable because several systems work together.
Slide 2: Cyclic — attitude/translation.
Slide 3: Collective — changes rotor thrust.
Slide 4: Pedals — anti-torque/yaw control.
Slide 5: Powertrain — converts engine power into useful rotor work.
Slide 6: Performance — aircraft capability depends on conditions.
Slide 7: Good pilots manage both aircraft and decisions.
