# Lesson 04 — Engines & Powerplants

## Editorial position
Explain the energy chain: engine → transmission/drivetrain → rotor system. Avoid treating “engine power” as a universal operational prescription. Turbine and piston helicopters differ substantially, and aircraft-specific procedures always win.

## Master video
**Working title:** How Helicopters Turn Engine Power Into Flight

**Hook:** The engine does not directly “lift the helicopter.” Power moves through a system that ultimately keeps the rotor system operating and producing useful aerodynamic force.

**Core beats:**
1. Engine types — piston and turbine powerplants are common in helicopter operations, with major differences in design and operation.
2. Transmission/drivetrain — transfers engine power to the rotor system and associated systems as designed.
3. Rotor RPM — helicopters are designed around a specified rotor operating range; maintaining it is fundamental to safe flight.
4. Power demand — aerodynamic and flight-condition changes can alter required power.
5. Temperature/pressure/engine indications — monitoring depends on the aircraft and powerplant.
6. Governor/control systems — some helicopters use systems that automatically help manage engine/rotor parameters; exact behavior is aircraft-specific.
7. Abnormal conditions — emergency response must come from the aircraft’s approved procedures and trained response, not a generic internet checklist.

## Short-form hooks
- “The engine does NOT directly lift the helicopter.”
- “Piston vs. turbine: two very different powerplants.”
- “Where does engine power go after combustion?”
- “Why does rotor RPM matter so much?”
- “Never memorize an emergency procedure from a random video.”

## Gauntlet seed
A learner notices an abnormal engine indication during a training scenario. The correct educational choice is to recognize the indication as a cue to use the aircraft-specific checklist/procedure and coordinate with the instructor, rather than improvise from generic advice.

## Quiz seeds
- What component transfers engine power to the rotor system?
- Why is rotor RPM monitoring critical?
- Why should engine indications be interpreted using aircraft-specific references?
- Why are piston and turbine powerplants taught differently?

## Oral-exam seed
“Trace the path of power from the engine to the main rotor.”

## Rights / safety gate
Do not copy manufacturer POH/RFM procedures, limitations, or emergency checklists. Cite authoritative references and write original educational explanations. Any operational procedure requires human aviation review.
