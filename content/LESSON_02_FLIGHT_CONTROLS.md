# Lesson 02 — Flight Controls

## Editorial position
Teach the control system as a coordinated system, not as four isolated levers. The FAA Helicopter Flying Handbook describes the cyclic, collective, and antitorque pedals as primary flight controls and explains their interaction in hover and flight. Exact control behavior varies by aircraft, so AEROSFORGE must never turn a general explanation into aircraft-specific operating instruction.

## Master video
**Working title:** Four Controls. One Helicopter. Can You Explain Them?

**Hook:** A helicopter does not have one control that simply means “up.” Every primary control changes something important, and changing one often creates a reason to coordinate another.

**Core beats:**
1. Cyclic — changes rotor thrust direction and therefore helicopter attitude/translation.
2. Collective — changes main-rotor blade pitch collectively and changes total rotor thrust.
3. Antitorque pedals — change tail-rotor thrust on conventional tail-rotor helicopters and control yaw/heading; configurations vary.
4. Power/throttle or engine control — aircraft-specific means of managing engine power/rotor RPM; do not generalize across helicopter designs.
5. Coordination — control inputs interact; the pilot continually monitors attitude, heading, altitude, airspeed, power, and aircraft response.
6. Trim/stability/augmentation — some helicopters add systems that change how controls feel or behave.
7. Instructor boundary — first-flight control manipulation belongs under qualified instruction in the aircraft being flown.

## Short-form hooks
- “Which helicopter control actually makes you go up?”
- “Four controls. If you confuse these, stop scrolling.”
- “Why does pulling collective change more than altitude?”
- “The pedals are not just helicopter rudder pedals.”
- “The biggest beginner mistake: treating each control like it works alone.”

## Gauntlet seed
**Scenario:** A student is asked to identify the purpose of each primary control before touching the controls.
**Best decision:** Explain the intended control functions, verify aircraft-specific procedures with the instructor/flight manual, and do not manipulate controls without instruction.

## Quiz seeds
- Which control changes the pitch of all main-rotor blades collectively?
- Which control primarily changes the direction of rotor thrust?
- What is the primary purpose of antitorque pedals on a conventional tail-rotor helicopter?
- Why can one control input require coordination with another?

## Oral-exam seed
“Explain cyclic, collective, and antitorque pedal functions to a non-pilot without using jargon.”

## Rights / safety gate
Create original explanations, examples, scripts, graphics, and scenarios. Use FAA material as a factual reference rather than copying its wording or diagrams. AEROSFORGE content is educational and does not replace the aircraft RFM/POH, regulations, or CFI instruction.
